In June and July 2026, the friends group took part in workshops with Paul Knights, a local writer, photographer and environmental educator, to explore the relationship between the history and ecology of Colden Clough and Lumb Bank. We looked at how farming, woodland management, quarrying, water management, industry and conservation have all helped shape the landscape and biodiversity we see today.

These small, conversational workshops allowed us to walk together through the landscape, stopping at particular habitats, ruins and landscape features, and we used old maps, archival photographs and other historical material to explore how the clough and its ecology and wildlife has changed over time.

## Public Walk

Participants in the workshops had the opportunity to help co-lead the public heritage walk on 5th July 2026, speaking at points along the route about the work of the group. Paul spoke about all the information he had collected for the project and brought resources for participants to look at.

## Project outcomes

One of the outcomes of Paul's project is an extensive piece of writing covering all the stops on the walk and showcasing photographs and historical maps - <a href="https://landscapestory.co.uk/heritage-detectives/" target="landscapestory">Heritage Detectives: Reading the Living Landscape of Colden Clough and Lumb Bank</a>.

Another was a map of the walk showing the route and the six stops we made, with some basic information and links to Paul's piece.
